# Arithmetic and Number Theory

## Overview
**Arithmetic** is the fundamental branch of mathematics concerning basic operations on numbers (addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division). **Number Theory** extends these concepts to study the properties, relationships, and structures of integers ($\mathbb{Z}$) and natural numbers ($\mathbb{N}$).

## Core Concepts

### 1. Divisibility and Prime Numbers
An integer $a$ divides an integer $b$ (written as $a \mid b$) if there exists an integer $k$ such that $b = a \cdot k$.
* **Prime Number:** An integer $p > 1$ whose only positive divisors are $1$ and $p$.
* **Composite Number:** An integer $n > 1$ that has positive divisors other than $1$ and itself.
* **Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic:** Every integer $n > 1$ can be represented as a unique product of prime numbers, up to the order of factors:
$$n = p_1^{e_1} p_2^{e_2} \cdots p_k^{e_k}$$

### 2. GCD and LCM
* **Greatest Common Divisor (GCD):** The largest positive integer that divides two or more integers, written as $\gcd(a, b)$.
* **Least Common Multiple (LCM):** The smallest positive integer divisible by two or more integers, written as $\operatorname{lcm}(a, b)$.
* **Relationship:** For any two positive integers $a$ and $b$:
$$\gcd(a, b) \times \operatorname{lcm}(a, b) = a \cdot b$$

### 3. Modular Arithmetic
A system of arithmetic for integers where numbers "wrap around" upon reaching a fixed quantity called the **modulus** $m$.
* **Congruence:** Two integers $a$ and $b$ are congruent modulo $m$ if their difference $a - b$ is an integer multiple of $m$:
$$a \equiv b \pmod{m} \iff m \mid (a - b)$$

## Key Terminology
* **Division Algorithm:** For any integer $a$ and positive integer $b$, there exist unique integers $q$ (quotient) and $r$ (remainder) such that $a = bq + r$ where $0 \le r < b$.
* **Coprime (Relatively Prime):** Integers $a$ and $b$ are coprime if $\gcd(a, b) = 1$.
* **Diophantine Equation:** An equation requiring integer solutions (e.g., $ax + by = c$).

## Relationships and Applications
* **Abstract Algebra:** Number theory motivates structural algebra, introducing algebraic structures like groups, rings (e.g., $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$), and fields.
* **Computer Science & Cryptography:** Provides the structural foundation for algorithms, pseudo-random number generation, and modern encryption systems like RSA.
